The ASR argument and expanded dissent sample

October 19 2026 · Research argument and evidence status

Revised Word manuscript (local file) · Live expanded-sample status · Reading scope and sources

The question

When does criticism remain a contribution to a political project, and when does it become a challenge to the critic's belonging? The proposed story concerns how audiences interpret and handle criticism. Efficacy objections question whether action can achieve a goal. Explicit withdrawal questions whether continued support is deserved. Those claims can invite a defense of action, an effort to restore commitment, or a demand for loyalty. Which response occurs may depend on whether the author already licenses the criticism and whether the critic expresses a tie to the project.

This is a proposed mechanism, not an established finding. Conditional acceptance of conduct is already a substantial sociological theme. Our contribution would need to specify how the commitment contested and its relation to a shared argument organize the interaction, then demonstrate that process with credible comparisons and close readings.

Illustrative statements above are invented examples, not quotations or observations from the sample. Actual categories can overlap.

A rebuttal alone does not demonstrate boundary enforcement. A claim that action remains worthwhile alone does not establish a broader process of restoring commitment unless it addresses contribution or the shared project. An unanswered comment remains in the denominator, but it supplies no reply content and cannot establish punishment. Tone, posting time, visibility, topic, author participation and repeated interactions remain alternative explanations.

Why these ASR papers matter

Childress and Friedkin 2012 make reception a process involving a common text, readers and group interaction. Their before-and-after design measures interpretive change; ours can observe public reception but cannot infer private attitude change. Their writing moves from competing explanations to a design that can distinguish them. We should do the same, with article–root–reply sequences serving the argument.

Hahl, Kim and Zuckerman Sivan 2018 begin with an apparent contradiction and distinguish mechanisms before testing them experimentally. They show why perceived illegitimacy can make norm violation appealing. For us, this motivates a rival to a simple severity or punishment account: radical criticism may affirm local commitments. It does not imply that newsletter critics are equivalent to electoral candidates.

Reilly 2018 is a particularly close theoretical comparison: ambiguous conduct is judged in relation to community authenticity and standing. We have only reviewed the published abstract and author summary, not its final full text. We cannot borrow ethnographic knowledge of membership that our data do not contain. Explicit claims of prior or retained support are observable ties; they are not proof of membership.

Lamont 2018 helps distinguish judgments of a person's worth and membership from agreement with a proposition. Kitts 2006 warns against assuming that norm enforcement necessarily benefits collective action. These connections motivate the question; they do not turn our preliminary reply differences into evidence of a mechanism.

What has actually been read

Six downloaded ASR articles received focused section-level review: Childress and Friedkin 2012; Hahl and colleagues 2018; Kitts 2006; DellaPosta 2020; Lamont 2018; and Simpson and colleagues 2026. The exact passages, versions and sources are documented in the reading review and ZIP manifest. This is not a claim of six cover-to-cover readings or an exhaustive literature review. Reilly 2018, Rawlings 2020 and Chen 2026 were reviewed at abstract/author-summary level only. AJS, Annual Review of Sociology and Social Forces supplements are not counted as ASR papers.

What the completed baseline shows

The original probability sample contains 4,268 comments, including 3,158 without external replies. Its completed provisional coding identifies ten primary efficacy cases and eight broad legitimacy cases. Only two of the latter explicitly withdraw support. Six efficacy roots and one legitimacy root receive an external reply within seven days. Weighting narrows the raw response gap from 47.5 to 10.5 percentage points. The types do not overlap within the same discussions, and the sole answered legitimacy root concerns billionaire power rather than an explicit departure. This is insufficient to claim that audiences punish withdrawal, accept futility, or maintain commitment through a particular mechanism.

The expanded draw

From a frozen frame of 297,889 eligible captured roots, excluding the original 4,268, an independent publication-stratified random baseline selects 4,827 roots. Independent draws within three positive lexical strata select another 7,710; 232 appear in both, giving 12,305 unique additional roots across 49 publications and 3,508 discussions. All roots have positive selection probability. Replies and reactions are excluded from selection and classification input. Exact seeded draws, probabilities, source hashes and input coverage passed an offline replay.

The baseline preserves screen-negative and unanswered comments. Enrichment improves retrieval of possible rare objections, but a lexical match is not dissent. The new schema separates explicit withdrawal, conditional support, denial of allied worthiness, denial of representation and institutional criticism. It retains licensed efficacy/legitimacy criticism separately from local dissent and records explicit prior or retained support without inferring private ideology. This frame is conditional on collected discussions; its weighted estimates will not stand for every historical Substack comment. Original and expanded designs remain separate.

The fixed draw is completed irrespective of response patterns. More labels do not guarantee power: distinct publications, discussions, narrow subtypes, answered roots, and boundary acts matter. Fresh input coding has no previous outcomes; assistant review nevertheless occurs with knowledge of earlier pilot findings. Source audits and independent human validation remain necessary. New outcomes will be joined only after a separate label freeze and source review. Classification stopped after 11,413 valid model assignments because the configured API account returned an exhausted credit balance. The model flags 193 primary efficacy and 89 broad legitimacy candidates; these unaudited, incomplete yields do not establish response differences. All 892 pending comment identifiers are saved, and automatic API retries have stopped. The manuscript includes this interim yield note while retaining the original response tables. Scraping continues independently. See the status page for the saved checkpoint.

What changed in the manuscript

The introduction now leads with the problem of correcting a shared undertaking while maintaining participation. Theory specifies the proposed responses and the article-licensed alternative. It states observations that would fail to support the account and separates response probability from content among answered roots. The sampling section documents the new draw, while the tables retain the completed original results. The draft is a 5,744-word working manuscript in ASR format. Formatting and a promising question do not yet make it an ASR-ready empirical paper.
