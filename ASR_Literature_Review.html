ASR literature and writing revision

October 19, 2026 · Six ASR articles downloaded and checked, plus three close articles reviewed at abstract level.

Download six-paper reading packet and source manifest (local file) · Revised Word manuscript (local file)

The argument to sharpen: Political criticism varies in the commitment it contests: an account of events, a course of action, or the worth of the political project. Whether it becomes dissent depends on its relation to the published argument. Whether it is treated as legitimate depends on respondents’ conduct. Neither can be inferred from negative sentiment alone.

Changes to the writing

The revision opens with the tension between correcting a shared project and calling that project into question. It develops an audience-contingent alternative to a simple hierarchy of punishable dissent, distinguishes attention from engagement and exclusion, and reports the completed random sample before discussing limits. Administrative collection history and older pilots move to measurement notes. Rare legitimacy and efficacy categories remain separate; sparse results are not converted into a confirmatory claim. All empirical data remain scraped Substack material.

Closest design: situated reception

Childress, C. Clayton, and Noah E. Friedkin. 2012. Cultural Reception and Production: The Social Construction of Meaning in Book Clubs. American Sociological Review 77(1):45–68. DOI

Their book-club study links individual interpretations, interpersonal influence, and group context, using evaluations before and after discussion. Our article–comment–reply sequence can examine how interpretations are publicly received. It cannot demonstrate attitude change without comparable before/after evidence. Write the contribution around a situated interaction, rather than a census of political opinions.

Closest political mechanism: legitimacy and audience judgment

Hahl, Oliver, Minjae Kim, and Ezra W. Zuckerman Sivan. 2018. The Authentic Appeal of the Lying Demagogue: Proclaiming the Deeper Truth about Political Illegitimacy. American Sociological Review 83(1):1–33. DOI

Their experiments show how perceived political illegitimacy can make a norm-violating candidate appear an authentic champion to an aggrieved audience. This supplies a rival to a simple punishment hypothesis: radical criticism can affirm local commitments when the author has already delegitimated the same institution. Separate criticism licensed by an article from a challenge to that article. Their candidate evaluations are not evidence about sanctions against newsletter commenters.

Read: Selected introduction, experimental logic, and discussion/conclusion passages, PDF pp. 1–2, 9–12, and 22–25.
Copy: Public mirror of publisher-formatted OnlineFirst reprint; title, authors, and DOI verified against publisher and author records. Mirror ownership is unverified.

Norms and collective action

Kitts, James A. 2006. Collective Action, Rival Incentives, and the Emergence of Antisocial Norms. American Sociological Review 71(2):235–59. DOI

The computational model shows that informal peer enforcement need not promote collective contribution; results depend on incentives, their rivalness, and cohesion. Do not assume that a response protecting unity benefits a political project. Distinguish the content of a norm from its presumed function. Our descriptive trace data do not test this incentive model.

Read: Scanned reprint visually inspected at journal pp. 235–36 and 253–54; model scope and discussion.
Copy: Author-hosted scanned published reprint; no machine-readable text layer.

Broader problem: cross-cutting disagreement

DellaPosta, Daniel. 2020. Pluralistic Collapse: The “Oil Spill” Model of Mass Opinion Polarization. American Sociological Review 85(3):507–36. DOI

Using repeated GSS surveys, this paper examines consolidation of belief alignments across domains. Its aggregate account leaves questions about local interactions and individual change open. Use that distinction to motivate observing disagreement within audiences. Our reply data cannot establish mass polarization, historical ideological change, or interpersonal persuasion.

Read: Selected introduction and discussion/limitations passages, journal pp. 507–8 and 526–28.
Copy: Published reprint provided by ASA.

Recognition and boundaries

Lamont, Michèle. 2018. Addressing Recognition Gaps: Destigmatization and the Reduction of Inequality. American Sociological Review 83(3):419–44. DOI

Recognition concerns culturally supported judgments of worth and membership, with institutions and repertoires shaping responses to stigmatization. This helps distinguish recognition of a critic as a participant from agreement with the critic’s claim. Do not equate being ignored in a thread with stigmatization, or a polite rebuttal with exclusion. The paper addresses broader inequalities than the present setting can measure.

Read: Selected opening argument and concluding research-agenda passages, journal pp. 419–22 and 430–32.
Copy: ASA-hosted JSTOR published reprint; DOI verified from PDF.

Recent extension: relational normative context

Simpson, Brent, et al. 2026. How Norms Depend on the Presence and Status of Observers: The Relational Contingency of Everyday Behaviors in 90 Societies. American Sociological Review 91(5):709–30. DOI

Responses from over 25,000 people in 90 societies show that normative judgments vary with observers’ presence and status. This motivates preserving observable respondent roles, such as a current-post byline match, instead of pooling every response. Those status judgments do not establish that a newsletter author causes conformity; our unmatched accounts are not verified peers.

Read: Selected abstract, design, and discussion passages, journal pp. 710, 715–17, and 724–25.
Copy: Published version, CC BY, Glasgow institutional repository; full author list is retained in the PDF.

Close articles without downloaded final full text

Reilly, Patrick. 2018. No Laughter among Thieves: Authenticity and the Enforcement of Community Norms in Stand-Up Comedy. American Sociological Review 83(5):933–58. Source
Published abstract and author summary reviewed; final full text not downloaded (publisher PDF returned 403). Community standing affects whether ambiguous conduct is treated as a violation. This is a close theoretical anchor for conditional reception. We cannot measure the ethnographic standing or authenticity that the study observes. A separate working paper marked do-not-circulate was not packaged as the published article.

Rawlings, Craig M. 2020. Cognitive Authority and the Constraint of Attitude Change in Groups. American Sociological Review 85(6):992–1021. Source
Publisher abstract reviewed; no full-text download. The study links group authority, relations, and attitude constraint. It directs attention to author–audience roles but cannot be tested by counting supportive replies alone.

Chen, Lingxiao. 2026. Performing Nationalism: Celebrity Politics and Audience Boundary Work Under Authoritarianism. American Sociological Review 91(3):405–34. Source
Publisher abstract reviewed; no full-text download. Its survey/interview account of audience boundary work cautions against reading outward alignment as uniform private conviction. It is contextual literature, not data added to this study.
