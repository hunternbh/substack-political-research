What the focal contrast actually says

Within seven days, 6 of 10 efficacy comments and 1 of 8 legitimacy comments receive an external reply. The raw gap is 47.5 percentage points, but first-phase weighting reduces it to 10.5 points (42.1% versus 31.6%). No discussion contains both primary types. The sole answered legitimacy root concerns billionaire power; all four coalition-legitimacy cases have no external reply. This cannot establish differential punishment, acceptance, or an insider-dissent effect.

How much coding is verified?

A pinned model classified every root using full article text, with replies, likes and previous labels withheld. A targeted assistant check covered 200 roots, including every model primary/secondary rare-type assignment and all prior rare cases. It changed eligibility or primary type in 31 cases. The checked root file was frozen before new response coding. Earlier pilot findings remained in the assistant’s context, so the assistant check was not fully outcome-naive.

All 1,951 direct replies were separately coded; 71 received assistant checks, including every model-flagged boundary/derogation act toward dissent candidates and every focal rare-case response. Most other replies remain model-only. These checks are not independent human validation. Model evidence snippets fail literal-match checks in 936 root and 728 article excerpts; unmatched snippets are not used as verified quotes. Actual source text is displayed below.

Two further possible input errors emerged during response reading. They remain flagged in the frozen main table and are omitted only in a separate sensitivity. We preserve these issues instead of claiming that a coding freeze guarantees accuracy. Full article/root validation packet without labels or outcomes (local file).

Engagement and boundary policing are different outcomes

Among 340 mature candidates, 72 receive support, 56 rebuttal and 30 clarification. Seven receive directed ridicule, five a personal insult, two an exclusionary response, one a political-disloyalty accusation and five a bad-faith attribution; outcomes overlap. Neither focal type receives a coded personal insult, exclusion or unity demand. One efficacy case receives ridicule and another bad-faith attribution. Those sparse observations cannot establish equal treatment or tolerance.

The positive-alignment restriction leaves only two efficacy and one legitimacy case. At current unweighted frequencies, one hundred cases of each type would require roughly 43,000 and 53,000 random roots, respectively, before validation, clustering or response rarity. These are planning extrapolations, not power calculations. A prospectively stratified rare-case sample, with a random baseline, known probabilities and screen-negative validation, is likely more efficient.

Capture-time text is another limitation: 274 roots carry an edit timestamp, including 20 edited after an observed external reply. Initial article and comment versions are not reconstructed. Excluding roots with a reported edit leaves 9 efficacy cases (6 replied) and 7 legitimacy cases (1 replied); the feasibility conclusion is unchanged.

The revised ASR argument

The draft now develops the boundary between repairing a common political project and questioning its worth. It connects that problem to cultural reception, authenticity, norm enforcement and recognition; then distinguishes attention, substantive uptake and explicit exclusion. Older pilots move out of the main results. Six downloaded ASR articles and three abstract-level close references have documented access/version notes. All empirical observations remain scraped Substack material.
