LABELING PILOT & EXPANDED SAMPLING DESIGN

What kinds of dissent draw what kinds of response?

82 root comments and 56 replies provisionally labeled. The response categories are observable; a systematic difference between dissent types is not yet established.

Scope decision: collect the current top 50 U.S. Politics paid-bestseller publications; preserve top-20 and original top-10 indicators. Use October 18, 2023–October 18, 2026 in New York time as the three-year calendar window, with a two-year subset beginning October 18, 2024. Do not select the preferred window or cohort size based on which produces a more attractive result.

Collection is continuing

The finite, resumable collector is running as a hidden local Python process. At the checkpoint 2026-10-19T03:40:51.158283+00:00, it had reached 50/50 publications, stored 16,433 article records and 13,551 comment records from 98 discussions in the new panel. These counts overlap earlier collections and must not be added to them. This report is a fixed checkpoint; the collection status page refreshes as work continues.

Archives are discovered round-robin across publications, then extended backward until the endpoint ends or an entire returned page precedes the time window. Publication errors are recorded as incomplete, not silently replaced by a different writer. Public bodies are retained; paid bodies are excluded. Preview discussions are separately marked. After archive discovery, the collector selects one eligible public-prose discussion per publication and calendar month using a fixed random seed and records the number of eligible posts in that stratum. This is a sample of discussions, not a complete comment census.

Why top 50 helps, and what it does not solve

Keeping the full top-50 frame now makes top-20 versus top-50 robustness checks possible without choosing extra publications after seeing the results. It adds communities, genres, and organizational forms. The unit is a publication, not necessarily an individual contributor; archived bylines are retained for later author-level checks.

The frame is today’s commercial-success list, not the historical top 50 throughout 2023–2026. It misses political writers categorized under News, World Politics, or other categories, and it is not designed to balance ideological groups. Rank on today’s paid leaderboard can depend on survival and growth during the study period. The defensible target population is discussion in these selected publications during the observed window, not all political Substack readership. Expanding from 20 to 50 does not by itself eliminate this selection problem.

What was actually labeled

The initial pilot uses the already collected ten-publication comment corpus. It has 60 randomly selected eligible root comments (six per publication within the purposively selected public-article discussions), plus 22 keyword-enriched cases that were required to have replies. All 82 fall inside both proposed windows; this is not yet a balanced historical sample. Their strata remain separate in the files.

The labels distinguish criticism type, agreement with the article, whether it is a clear author challenge or only a possible coalition critique, and tone. Fifty-six replies from 33 roots were coded, using at most the earliest three direct replies available in each context packet. Cases with no replies are retained. Outcome flags mean observed in this inspected early-reply set; they are not an exhaustive label of every later response.

Label provenance: one assistant, provisional coding. Input packets omitted reactions/replies, and reply packets omitted the criticism labels and reactions. However, some cases had been seen in the earlier feasibility review, so this was not a fully blinded independent exercise. There is no human reliability score or validated classifier accuracy. Each label has a case identifier and rationale. The protocol labels expressed text, not ordinary commenters’ inferred party membership.

Pooling those strata to estimate whether dissent generates more responses would be invalid. Silence is not evidence of rejection. No p-values or causal estimates are reported.

The narrower loyal-criticism check

Only eight cases in this pilot clearly combine criticism with an expressed shared political goal or defense of the broader cause. This is a content-based eligibility screen, not a finding about the person’s private affiliation. It is much narrower than counting every disagreement with a writer as in-group dissent.

How the next sample should answer the question

  1. Use the calendar frame. Sample public-prose discussions within publication-months across the three-year window. Report missing months, publication launch dates, archive gaps, migration dates, and unavailable comments. Keep satire, digests, videos, and multiwriter outlets identifiable.
  2. Sample roots independently of responses. Within each selected discussion, draw a reproducible random sample of roots, retaining no-reply cases. Keep a separately enriched challenge set for discovering rare dissent and testing labels; do not use its raw response frequencies as population estimates.
  3. Validate alignment and response target. Confirm that criticism concerns an actor/position aligned with the article or explicitly shares its political goal. Distinguish author-licensed criticism, cross-position opposition, factual correction, and wording-only disagreement. Code whether a reply addresses the root, a sibling, a quoted speaker, or a politician.
  4. Separate outcomes. Measure support, reasoned rebuttal, ridicule, personal insult, explicit unity/exclusion language, and likes separately. A factual correction is not punishment; a slur aimed at a politician is not a sanction against the commenter.
  5. Use paired robustness checks. Estimate the same predeclared comparison for top 20 and top 50, then for two and three years, with publication/article controls and clustering appropriate to repeated participants. Repeated snapshots or fixed-age restrictions are needed before interpreting differences in cumulative likes. The sample-size decision should follow labeled event frequencies, not the total scraped comment count.

Assessment: the expanded study is worth pursuing, and labeling is now underway. The current pilot gives us workable categories and a specific hypothesis about discouraging demobilizing pessimism. It does not yet establish that one kind of dissent systematically receives harsher responses.
