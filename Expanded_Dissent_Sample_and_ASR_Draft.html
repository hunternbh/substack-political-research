What the expanded sample tells us

There are now substantially more identifiable disagreements to study. The sample supports a descriptive comparison of factual, editorial, and tactical criticism. It does not yet support a reliable seven-type comparison: legitimacy challenges number only six, policy and efficacy cells remain small, and direct exclusion is absent from the coded replies. The stronger paper is about how disagreement becomes argument, clarification, or accommodation; a loyalty-punishment claim remains unestablished.

Keep the denominators separate. All 4,268 roots were randomly drawn without using their reply outcomes. The 554 reviewed roots are a second probability sample enriched for possible dissent, including unflagged comments. The other 3,714 are unreviewed. The 672 screening hits are not dissent labels. Labels are provisional judgments by one assistant, with no independent human validation.

What the replies contain

All 63 direct reply texts attached to the dissent candidates were coded after the input labels were frozen. Five are self-follow-ups; 58 are from other identifiers. Nine of those 58 match current-post bylines. Other identifiers are not verified peers. Across the full window, 22 roots receive support, 21 rebuttal, and ten clarification. No explicit root-directed exclusion, unity policing, ridicule or personal insult was coded; one bad-faith accusation and one conditional boundary warning are retained separately. This does not establish an absence of hostility elsewhere, in deleted material, or deeper in reply chains.

Several useful exchanges involve clarifying whether a disagreement exists. An author says a qualification was already in the article; another explains an apparent provocation was a joke. A reader objects to treating a violent image as humor and receives agreement. These observations make context and the distinction between rebuttal and exclusion central to the ASR argument.

What is still too thin

Only five discussions contain sampled dissent candidates of different primary types. Most comparisons would therefore also compare different articles or communities. Combined sampling and annotation weights are highly concentrated: the candidates' Kish effective sample size is approximately 12.4, a diagnostic of weight concentration rather than a design-adjusted power calculation. Neither thousands of scraped comments nor 118 candidates solves that problem.

The next empirical step is independent full-context validation, continued annotation of the 3,714 remaining roots, and additional probability sampling within discussions to obtain multiple types under the same article. Any targeted search for rare cases should remain a separate, probability-documented supplement. Prevalence estimates must retain the random denominator and account for both sampling stages.

Collection coverage and checks

The frozen cohort is the current paid US Politics top fifty publications, including brands with multiple contributors. The public-prose draw covers 49: The Weekly Dish has no eligible public-prose post in this frame. The window is October 18, 2023–October 18, 2026 in New York; the nested two-year post subset contains 3,334 roots. Article eligibility is based on outlet category and public prose, so off-topic discussions remain visible and are separately annotated.

All 898 selected discussions were processed, 894 supplied roots, and no request error occurred in this draw. Seed replay passed for every month, post, root and annotation selection; inverse probabilities were recomputed. All sampled root post IDs match their source articles, all 4,268 texts are distinct, and none is marked suppressed. One sampled root reports a child-list gap; it is not a coded dissent candidate. Public responses cannot establish complete historical visibility.
