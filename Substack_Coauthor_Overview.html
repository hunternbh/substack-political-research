POLITICAL SUBSTACK RESEARCH · WORKING PROJECT · OCTOBER 19, 2026

What we have, and what we can study

We have a large archive of political newsletters and their public conversations. It lets us study how readers try to steer writers, which kinds of criticism other readers support, and whether writers’ later posts respond to that pressure.

We have not yet shown that reader pressure changes a writer’s views. This page separates the data we already hold from the work still underway.

The exact comment total is 1,256,544; 1,238,401 records contain text. We do not have subscriber payment histories, reading/view counts, or evidence that a writer saw a particular comment. Public discussions may be incomplete. Text access is also uneven: about 99% of post records for The Free Press and The Huckabee Post have restricted bodies, while some other publications have none. Comparisons between writers must account for this.

What is the political mix?

The original fifty are heavily liberal or left-leaning. Our provisional reading places 39 broadly left/liberal, 1 moderate liberal/center, 3 right/center-right, 4 mixed, 2 with conservative origins but unclear current policy positions, and 1 unclassified. Some labels are tentative.

Anti-Trump does not automatically mean left-wing: Charlie Sykes is a center-right example. A publication calling itself independent or moderate does not automatically mean its policy views are centrist. We have not yet classified the additional fifty.

What the pilot tells us so far

The latest manual expansion reviewed 118 starting comments across 24 publications: 117 reader comments and one staff message. It included 86 reader comments with no captured direct reply within seven days, so the pilot does not focus only on arguments that attracted responses.

It contains examples of readers challenging a writer’s political loyalties, factual standards or editorial choices. This shows that the proposed question is observable in the archive. The samples are still too small and differently selected to establish which demand wins out, how common it is, or whether writers change because of it.
