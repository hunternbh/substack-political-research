POLITICAL SUBSTACK RESEARCH · WORKING PROJECT · OCTOBER 19, 2026

What we have, and what we can study

We have a large archive of political newsletters and their public conversations. It lets us study how readers try to steer writers, which kinds of criticism other readers support, and whether writers’ later posts respond to that pressure.

We have not yet shown that reader pressure changes a writer’s views. This page separates the data we already hold from the work still underway.

Find author replies before paying for interpretation

The full retained archive now has a Python search index. Exact account matching finds 8,569 direct replies to reader comments from listed article-author accounts. A broader regex search builds a first review queue of 3,740 comments, including a separately marked 1,200-comment random baseline. Of those baseline comments, 871 have no captured response anywhere below them within seven days.

Regex retrieves candidates; it does not label political loyalty. Local tests of newer Qwen and Gemma models still show substantial trade-offs between missing cases and overcalling them. We should validate interpretations on the smaller queue before considering a large annotation run. No paid model calls were used.

Latest manual pilot: how writers answer political pressure

116 fresh comments from 31 publications reviewed manually. The sample emphasizes actual author replies while keeping a separate 32-comment random baseline. Twenty-four baseline comments have no captured response at any depth within seven days. The review includes nested conversations and all six retained right/conservative publications, alongside left/liberal and centrist comparisons.

Three clear writer-directed loyalty disputes now have direct author replies. Sykes points to his campaigning for Harris; Williamson argues that criticism helps the shared electoral goal; Wonkette’s editor distinguishes disagreement from abuse. Thirty other comments make ordinary criticisms, which also receive both substantive engagement and sharp rebukes. These small, deliberately enriched counts do not establish that loyalty demands receive harsher treatment.

The stronger story is a dispute over what responsible political writing requires. Writers may defend independent judgment, claim they are helping the coalition, or police the terms of discussion. The Wonkette episode includes an editor’s self-report of cancellations and another reader’s offer of support; verified payments and audience-caused stance changes remain unobserved. Only two of 28 deeper author-reply links clearly continue an answer to the starting comment; most answer someone else.

All focal comments and immediate parents were read in full, with saved article leads and relevant passages plus full prose for the central disputes. One assistant coded this pilot; independent validation is outstanding. No paid APIs, external model calls or local model inference; new API spend $0.
