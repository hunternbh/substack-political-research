We can locate author replies directly

8,581 reply pairs match an account listed in the article’s byline. Of these, 8,569 answer a reader account rather than another known publication author; 8,558 of those reader pairs occur within seven days. The listed-author replies occur in 1,715 saved discussions. Another 173 reply pairs match accounts marked is_primary in captured publication metadata; these are recorded separately, not silently treated as the article’s author. That metadata flag is not proof of a sole editor or publisher.

Every pair connects the saved reply account to its parent comment and source article. I independently checked all 9,402 pairs across the listed-author, primary-account and other-byline categories against the original saved records. The graph results reproduce all 360 direct/descendant response-presence classifications from the latest pilot, with no writer-presence differences.

Why this matters for the Trump-supporting cases

The saved discussions contain eleven direct reader replies by Stone across five posts, none by Loury, and 221 by Kelly across seventeen posts. This describes the finite captured discussions. It does not establish their overall responsiveness or prove a writer saw an unanswered comment. There are many more matched reply opportunities elsewhere in the retained cohort, but we must account for who habitually replies.

A smaller, mixed review queue

The broader regex retrieves 78,218 candidate reader comments for searching, not final classification. The initial review queue contains 3,740 unique comments across 1,900 posts: a response-blind random baseline, text-enriched cases, and a separately marked arm of regex candidates with author participation. Arms overlap. We should interpret this queue before considering a large annotation run.

The random baseline contains 1,200 comments—twenty per publication, drawn without using regex hits, likes or reply outcomes. It includes 426 nested comments and 871 with no captured response at any depth within seven days. Thirty-two have a direct matched author reply and thirty-three have matched author participation somewhere below them. Equal numbers per publication do not form a simple random sample of all platform comments.

The Python-first workflow

  1. Match accounts. Use the same saved account-ID hash scheme as the collection and the article’s captured byline IDs. Keep primary-account and other-publication-byline matches separate. A matching display name alone is insufficient.
  2. Connect the conversation. Follow parent IDs within each post. One edge is a direct reply; two or more is deeper participation. Exclude self-replies, deleted or empty text, and invalid time order. Flag incomplete branches and immature follow-up windows.
  3. Search text. Regex families retrieve possible political restraint, loyalty, criticism, evidence challenges, independence, subscription exit and comment gatekeeping. A broader political-critique family catches criticism expressed without words such as “loyal” or “traitor.” Affordability and care context remain separate flags.
  4. Preserve the comparison. Keep a random baseline, including comments without responses. Text-only enrichment is selected independently of outcomes. Cases selected because an author replied have their own arm and cannot estimate an unconditional response effect.
  5. Read only what needs interpretation. Give an assistant, human coder or validated local model the article, focal comment, parent and relevant response. Classify the demand and actual response; do not infer concession from reply presence.

The first narrow regex missed important cases. It retrieved ten of the nineteen strict loyalty comments in the two own-side pilots. Adding broader political-critique retrieval raises that to nineteen of nineteen. Because those cases informed the change, this is calibration, not independent recall validation. A fresh manually reviewed random sample is still needed to find misses.

Some candidates will be ordinary policy criticism, praise, guest complaints or discussions of other outlets. Regex is intended to reduce the reading workload while leaving the final meaning open. It does not assign our substantive loyalty labels.

Do newer local models solve the interpretation problem?

I downloaded and tested Qwen3.5 9B and Gemma 4 12B locally. Both have Apache 2.0 model licenses. Their quantized weights fit this machine for these short text requests. The installed runtime was too old for them, so the tests used a temporary workspace runtime with cloud features disabled. The existing Ollama installation and stopped paid jobs were preserved.

For comparability, every model received the same 127 diagnostic cases, article prose, immediate-parent context, frozen prompt and schema. Thinking was disabled. A recheck of the original Qwen3 8B on the new runtime helps separate runtime changes from model changes. No reference labels appeared in prediction prompts and the prompt was not tuned after seeing these predictions.

These are trade-offs, not a validated winner. The newer Qwen reduces some extra flags but misses more reference loyalty cases. Gemma produces fewer extra flags but misses most reference cases. These results do not justify replacing manual checking with either model’s unreviewed labels. Other model sizes, reasoning modes, task decomposition or example-based prompts could behave differently and require their own tests.

The reference is one assistant’s provisional manual review, not independently adjudicated human truth. The set deliberately includes difficult positives and near misses; these percentages are not population accuracy estimates. Distinguishing writer from peer, cohost or guest remains essential even when a broad loyalty flag is correct.

What still requires evidence and judgment

A matched author reply may defend the original argument, correct a reader, express sympathy, thank someone or promise future coverage. A reply deeper in a branch may answer somebody else. Neither one establishes that audience pressure changed a later article.

The source window remains October 18, 2023–October 18, 2026. This screening uses only the sixty retained publications and the existing finite captures. It does not recover restricted prose, private replies, missing comments or actual payment transactions. There are 290 saved nodes reporting more children than returned. Baseline eligibility requires mature seven-day capture, 5–800 words, an unmatched reader account, and public saved prose of at least 150 words.

The right next step is to validate a small interpreted sample drawn from this queue, with independent human checking, then choose a model or manual workflow. The model should not perform joins, count replies, guess author identities or label every comment just to locate the discussions.
