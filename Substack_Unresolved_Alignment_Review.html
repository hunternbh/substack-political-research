Zeteo

Left / progressive; editorial tendency across contributors · High confidence

The larger public-text review includes affirmative economic-progressive advocacy for worker organizing and rejection of corporate influence, defenses of public healthcare coverage, and immigration analysis critical of restrictionism. This supports a broad progressive editorial tendency rather than merely an anti-Trump label. Zeteo publishes multiple authors and explicitly disclaims that each opinion is the organization’s own; do not assign every contributor the same ideology.

Democrats Will Keep Losing Until They Cut Ties With Billionaires and Corporations (2024-12-05). A published opinion piece argues for working-class representation, unions, expanded public provision and removal of corporate influence from Democratic politics. Author: Alexandra Rojas; this is an op-ed, not an official statement by every contributor. Read: Full saved public text.

Republicans Want You to Pay More for Healthcare (2025-12-09). Andrew Perez’s report/commentary treats loss of public healthcare subsidies and exclusions for abortion and gender-related care negatively. Read: Full saved public text.

Trump and Republicans Are Lying About Immigration and the Border (2025-01-16). John Harwood criticizes restrictive immigration narratives while using a libertarian expert; this corroborates editorial range but does not convert the quoted expert’s ideology into the outlet’s. Read: Full saved public text.

Silver Bulletin

Moderate liberal / classical-liberal and libertarian-leaning · Medium confidence

Silver directly calls himself a moderate liberal and favors individual rights, free speech, decentralized decision-making and markets. A later essay explicitly places this form of liberalism closer to the center than the left. For a coarse three-way description, center/moderate liberal is the best working category. This does not mean neutral reporting, an exact ideological midpoint, or absence of liberal issue positions. Coding Silver as center-left is a reasonable sensitivity rather than calling him progressive-left or Republican.

Why liberalism and leftism are increasingly at odds (2023-12-12). First-person moderate-liberal identification and preference for decentralized mechanisms and individual rights; selected passages read, not a claim to a complete current policy score. Read: Opening and selected argument passages.

Don't mistake Democratic partisan orthodoxy for a "coherent" philosophy (2024-12-11). Distinguishes classical/European liberalism from leftism and places the former closer to the ideological center. Read: Opening and selected argument passages.

Is the Democratic Party dominated by progressives or by centrists? (2025-10-10). Analyzes progressive and centrist Democratic factions without treating either faction’s views as his own. Read: Opening and selected argument passages.

Civil Discourse with Joyce Vance

Center-left / liberal; civil-rights and labor emphasis · High confidence

The reviewed Labor Day essay rejects Reagan’s small-government economic program, affirms organized labor and civil-rights work, and urges electoral action. The poll-tax essay defends expansive voting access and objects to exclusionary requirements. This is substantive liberal framing, not simply a biography as an Obama appointee. A detailed economic platform remains unmeasured.

McMahon’s own public prose rejects firearm-worship arguments and asks that children not have access to guns, advocates a constitutional amendment limiting money in politics, and criticizes religious-political curriculum mandates. Fiscal policy and many other domains remain unmeasured. An independent gubernatorial guest’s self-description is not McMahon’s.

Interpretation

The expanded review resolves eleven broad placements but does not produce complete policy scores. The new summary is 39 broad left/liberal, one center/moderate liberal, three right/center-right, four mixed/heterodox, two conservative-origin cases whose current policy range remains unresolved, and one unclassified publication. Moving Silver to the broad liberal group would make that 40 and zero in the first two categories. Leaving the more inferential McMahon and Morris labels unresolved would instead give 37 broad left/liberal and three unclassified publications. These sensitivity choices do not make the cohort politically balanced.

The important correction is Sykes: we now have an additional identifiable center-right commentator with substantial captured discussion, rather than only Huckabee among the previously classified current-right publications with comments. Pro-Trump right coverage is still confined to Huckabee in these confirmed classifications. Anti-Trump coalition membership is not a left ideology.

No private party affiliation, transactions or beliefs are inferred. A quoted statement by a guest or source is distinguished from the author’s position; medical, legal, election and other factual assertions in the articles were not independently adjudicated in this political-orientation review. The two conservative-origin cases outside this twelve-publication task and the four earlier mixed classifications were not re-reviewed here.
