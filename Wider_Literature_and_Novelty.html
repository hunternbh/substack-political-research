Wider literature and the limits of the novelty claim

October 19, 2026 · Public searches and documents only · No paid model API calls

All six requested citations are incorporated substantively into the manuscript and reference list. The broad accounts of loyal dissent, sensitivity to criticism, betrayal, and public exit already exist. The pilot’s morale direction also enters a developed literature. Its potential lies in explaining how claims to respite and obligations to persist are negotiated, if a larger study establishes that process.

Seven publicly available PDFs and review notes (local file) · Pilot comparison · Manuscript

The six requested precedents

Packer2008

Already distinguishes dissent seeking improvement from disengagement. Strong identification can motivate criticism; type of criticism cannot reveal attachment by itself.

Packer, Dominic J. 2008. “On Being Both With Us and Against Us: A Normative Conflict Model of Dissent in Social Groups.” Personality and Social Psychology Review 12(1):50–72. https://doi.org/10.1177/1088868307309606.
Abstract and verified bibliographic record; public mirror PDF download timed out.

Hornsey2004

Perceived investment already explains differential reception of criticism. A benefit for insiders cannot be inferred merely from posting in the same newsletter.

Hornsey, Matthew J., Mark Trembath, and Sasha Gunthorpe. 2004. “‘You Can Criticize Because You Care’: Identity Attachment, Constructiveness, and the Intergroup Sensitivity Effect.” European Journal of Social Psychology 34(5):499–518. https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.212.
Publisher abstract and metadata; PDF returned 403.

Travaglino2014

Departure and joining a rival are distinct. Code destination and prior support separately from whether a discontinuity is completed, declared, conditional, or merely advocated.

Travaglino, Giovanni A., Dominic Abrams, Georgina Randsley de Moura, José M. Marques, and Isabel R. Pinto. 2014. “How Groups React to Disloyalty in the Context of Intergroup Competition: Evaluations of Group Deserters and Defectors.” Journal of Experimental Social Psychology 54:178–87. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesp.2014.05.006.
Repository abstract and accepted-manuscript opening, PDF pages 1–4. Public PDF saved.

Reiman2023

Party criticism and insults already connect insider benefit and black-sheep rejection. These depend on the comparison being made; punishment of political dissent is not a new central discovery.

Reiman, Anna Kaisa, and Tina C. Killoran. 2023. “When Group Members Dissent: A Direct Comparison of the Black Sheep and Intergroup Sensitivity Effects.” Journal of Experimental Social Psychology 104:104408. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesp.2022.104408.
Institutional abstract and metadata. No full-text PDF obtained.

Montanaro2019

Publicly justified refusal can contest claims to representation and demand accountability. An announcement of departure is not automatically this richer form of exit, nor proof of completed departure.

Montanaro, Laura. 2019. “Discursive Exit.” American Journal of Political Science 63(4):875–87. https://doi.org/10.1111/ajps.12463.
Publisher abstract plus institutional-manuscript introduction, PDF pages 1–4. Public manuscript saved.

Loustau2026

Political dissenters’ self-understood loyalty and fellow partisans’ judgments already diverge. A new paper must explain negotiations among particular obligations, beyond relabeling dissent as loyalty.

Loustau, Trystan, and Liane Young. 2026. “Redefining Loyalty: How Political Deviants Maintain Positive Self-Views Amid Ingroup Rejection.” Behavioral Sciences 16(1):126. https://doi.org/10.3390/bs16010126.
Abstract and bibliographic checks; author-laboratory PDF saved.

Additional foundations and close studies

Rao2022

Shows how economic decisions constitute relationships and obligations. For this setting, payment must be linked to a stated relational expectation; generic subscription mentions are insufficient.

Rao, Aliya Hamid. 2022. “Relational Work in the Family: The Gendered Microfoundation of Parents’ Economic Decisions.” American Sociological Review 87(6):1094–1120. https://doi.org/10.1177/00031224221132295.
Abstract and opening argument, PDF pages 2–3; institutional PDF saved.

Zelizer2012

Relational work offers an account of how payments and relationships are matched. It does not predict that money always gives a customer control.

Zelizer, Viviana A. 2012. “How I Became a Relational Economic Sociologist and What Does That Mean?” Politics & Society 40(2):145–74. https://doi.org/10.1177/0032329212441591.
Abstract and opening, institutional working-paper PDF pages 2–4; PDF saved.

Hochschild1979

The social regulation of feelings is a foundational precedent, not a novel finding. Text can show displayed expectations and evaluations; it does not reveal unexpressed feeling.

Hochschild, Arlie Russell. 1979. “Emotion Work, Feeling Rules, and Social Structure.” American Journal of Sociology 85(3):551–75. https://doi.org/10.1086/227049.
Publisher abstract and metadata; no new full-text PDF.

Jasper2011

Emotion’s role in mobilization is established and varies across emotions and processes. Do not assume that hope always helps or anger always hurts participation.

Jasper, James M. 2011. “Emotions and Social Movements: Twenty Years of Theory and Research.” Annual Review of Sociology 37:285–303. https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-soc-081309-150015.
Publisher/author abstract and metadata; publisher PDF returned 403.

Spelman2026

Shows that expected sanctions can exceed observed reactions. Our visible-comment archive cannot measure anticipated rejection, unvoiced dissent, or private belief change.

Spelman, Trevor, Abdo Elnakouri, Nour Kteily, and Eli J. Finkel. 2026. “Overestimating the Social Costs of Political Belief Change.” Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, accepted preprint. https://doi.org/10.1037/pspi0000516.
Accepted-preprint abstract and opening, PDF pages 1–3; PDF saved. Final-publication metadata not assumed.

Gokmenoglu2025

Especially close to the morale idea: dystopian forecasts can sustain action through collective work on hope and time. Mere observation that activists reject despair would add little.

Gokmenoglu, Birgan, and Gabriela Manley. 2025. “Hope and Time Work in Dystopian Contexts: Future-Oriented Temporalities of Activism in Post-Referendum Scotland and Turkey.” Time & Society 34(1):57–76. https://doi.org/10.1177/0961463X231191614.
Publisher abstract and introduction; accepted manuscript saved. Online publication 2023, final issue 2025.

Zilberstein (2022). The Tow Center’s Substack career study already discusses writer autonomy, direct audience accountability, and constraints. Read report summary and relevant sections; not an ASR article.

Hewa and Cohen (2025; online 2024). Controlled Connection: Substack and Writers of Color, Communication, Culture and Critique 18(1):113–120, DOI 10.1093/ccc/tcae042. Abstract reviewed; bounded audiences and safety are already studied.

Chen (2026). Performing Nationalism, ASR 91(3):405–434. Abstract reviewed; convergent public alignment can reflect different interpretations. We cannot infer shared conviction from comments.

Konechni (2026). When Political Pivots Shift Behaviors but Not Beliefs, ASR 91(2):256–285. Publisher and institutional abstract reviewed; supports separating observed behavior from belief.

Gould (2009). Moving Politics: Emotion and ACT UP’s Fight against AIDS. Publisher description and author bibliography checked, not the full book. Political emotion and sustaining activism have substantial prior scholarship.

Skilling, Carroll, and Lips-Wiersma (2025). Sustainability Leadership and the Governmentality of Hope, Organization Studies, online article. Publisher passages reviewed; competing discourses can treat hope as governing conduct or despair as empowering. Exact final issue metadata not assumed.

Search scope and remaining novelty work

Searches covered normative conflict, intergroup sensitivity and black-sheep effects, desertion/defection, discursive exit, political belief change, news-comment social control, Substack work and audience relationships, relational economic sociology, feeling rules, movement emotions, and political hope. The new pilot prompted additional searches combining hope/despair with feeling rules and collective action. Publisher and institutional records verified the six citations. Download manifests distinguish obtained copies from timeouts and access denials; no purchase or access bypass was used.

This is a wider exploratory search, not proof that the proposed process has never been studied. A full novelty assessment must still search bounded disengagement, activist burnout and care, the distribution of movement obligations, relational labor and patronage, and event-based audience change. In particular, the emotions literature rules out claiming the discovery that hope sustains activism. The appropriate next question is whether these newsletter exchanges reveal a sufficiently general and previously unexplained allocation of obligations and exemptions.
